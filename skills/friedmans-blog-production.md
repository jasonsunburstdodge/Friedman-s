# Friedman's blog production

Use this skill whenever a Friedman's Home Improvement blog post is planned, written or revised, including the twice-weekly scheduled run. It covers topic selection through a review-ready draft. Product picks and the project estimator come from `friedmans-product-estimator`. Staging, approval and publishing come from `friedmans-sana-release`. Load `friedmans-brand-guidelines` first; its name, voice, claims, color, type and imagery rules apply to everything here.

## What Friedman's is

See `friedmans-brand-guidelines` sections 1 and 2 for the company facts, store list and brand values (The Human Side of Home Improvement, Greener Choice, People Helping People). Customers are North Bay homeowners and local contractors. Confirm current hours, services and events before mentioning them.

## Cadence and post types

- **Tuesday, Seasonal overview.** What North Bay homes and gardens need right now: planting windows, first rains, frost, fire season, holidays, summer water use.
- **Friday, DIY how-to.** One project a homeowner can finish in a few hours with stated tools, time and skill level.
- Rotate departments and topics across weeks: garden, hardware, paint, plumbing, electrical (simple only), lumber and building materials, outdoor living, Pro Desk topics for contractors.
- Every post ends with a project estimator, even seasonal ones. Choose topics that lead to a real project.

## Step 1: Choose the topic

Score candidates on these signals and record the evidence for each:

1. **Season and weather.** North Bay seasonal calendar plus the 7-day forecast for Santa Rosa, Petaluma, Sonoma and Ukiah.
2. **What people are asking.** Current search and AI-answer questions from Google Trends, Search Console (when access exists), People Also Ask, or an equivalent. Never invent search volume or trend data. If a tool is unavailable, say which substitute was used.
3. **Inventory depth.** The core products for the project must be stocked at all four stores (check through `friedmans-product-estimator`). Do not choose a topic whose essential products are thin or missing at any store.
4. **Promotions and events.** Current promotions, KidsWorks, demos and workshops, when supplied. Never invent an event.
5. **History.** Check the editorial register for the last 12 weeks. Avoid repeating a topic, product set or opening within that window unless the season makes it new.

Produce two candidate titles. Pick the stronger one and add the other to the backlog in the register.

## Step 2: Prewriting checkpoint (must pass before prose)

Record a PASS or REVISE for each, with the supporting notes, in the article's planning record:

- **PREP-1 Audience question.** The reader (homeowner, renter, new homeowner, contractor), their likely skill level, the specific question they are asking, and the search intent.
- **PREP-2 Working title.** 10 to 15 words, plain language, an accurate promise. Count the words.
- **PREP-3 Outline.** H2/H3 outline with the purpose of each section, where safety notes go, and where product callouts belong (at the step where the product is used).

If the topic or main argument changes later, return to the affected step.

## Step 3: Assign the author

Authors are Friedman's department associates who have agreed to be bylined, with approved bios and photos. Keep the roster in the register: name, department, store, approved bio, approved photo, consent date.

- Match the post to the department that would actually answer the customer's question at the counter.
- **Byline-swap test:** if another department's associate could sign it unchanged, sharpen the angle around what this department knows.
- Never invent experience, quotes, years in the trade, certifications or anecdotes for an author. First-person tips must be general advice in their voice, not claimed personal events, unless the author supplied the story.
- If no approved author exists for the department, use "The Friedman's [Department] Team" and flag it. In demos, use the fictional department roster in `friedmans-brand-guidelines` section 11, labeled as fictional.
- End the author box with an offer to book a free 15-minute consultation with that author.

## Step 4: Write

Voice: follow `friedmans-brand-guidelines` section 3, a friendly, knowledgeable Friedman's team member talking to a neighbor. Plain words, direct address, contractions, short paragraphs, varied sentence length. Helpful, never pushy.

Writing rules (adapted from the SilverXis standard):

1. **Lead with the useful point.** The title and first two sentences say what the reader will be able to do or decide.
2. **Open in a way that fits the topic**: a seasonal moment, a common problem, a quick test the reader can do. Do not use the same opening every week.
3. **Explain before naming.** Describe what a product or technique does before using trade terms (kerf, backflow preventer, R-value).
4. **Check assumptions.** Do not assume the reader owns tools, owns the home, or knows how to measure. State what the advice depends on.
5. **Make each step explicit.** Numbered steps for sequences; one action per step; say how to know the step worked.
6. **Stay with one example** long enough to show the whole job, start to finish.
7. **Keep every section on the promise.** Remove tangents. If cutting a paragraph loses nothing, cut it.
8. **Qualify where it matters**, once, next to the claim (climate zone, door type, local code).
9. **Headings a scanner understands.** Lists only for genuinely parallel items or ordered steps.
10. **Specific, relevant ending.** Point to the project estimator and, where choices are involved, the free pro walkthrough. No generic service menu.
11. **House style.** AP punctuation. Do not use em dashes or en dashes as punctuation; rewrite the sentence. Ranges use "to."
12. **Never put prices, stock levels or sale terms in article copy.** Those render live from Sana.

**Plan, do, why.** Every post opens with a "Plan" section: what to check, measure or decide before starting, plus time and tools for DIY posts. Each major task after that gets its own section that explains why it matters (the problem it prevents or the result it produces) before how to do it. Close with how to check the work or what to expect next.

Length: seasonal 550 to 950 words; DIY 550 to 1,000 words including the steps.

## Step 5: Safety, accuracy and trust

- Follow manufacturer instructions and current local code. For gas, panel or circuit electrical, structural, roofing at height and anything permit-dependent, say plainly to hire a licensed professional, and offer the Pro Desk for referrals.
- Include a safety callout in every DIY post when the work involves blades, ladders, chemicals, power tools, electricity or water shutoffs.
- Keep a **fact ledger** for every material claim: claim, source URL or document, date checked, scope. Prefer manufacturer documentation, UC Master Gardeners and UC ANR, county and city sites, and code references. Omit or qualify anything you cannot verify.
- No invented customer stories, results, reviews, testimonials or staff quotes.

## Step 6: Callouts

Each post carries:

- **Product callouts** at the steps where products are used (2 to 4), supplied by `friedmans-product-estimator`.
- **One safety callout** for DIY posts (see above).
- **Optionally one local tip** that only a North Bay store would know, verified (frost dates, free sandbag sites, water district rebates).

Every callout must add something the paragraph next to it did not. No "Did you know?" filler.

## Step 7: Search, AEO and images

Deliver for each post:

- H1, SEO title (about 50 to 60 characters), meta description (about 150 to 160), slug, excerpt, category, 3 to 5 tags.
- A 40 to 60 word **direct answer** near the top that fully answers the main question.
- 2 to 4 related questions the post genuinely answers.
- Internal links: the relevant product category pages, store pages, and 1 to 3 related posts that exist. Never invent a URL.
- Schema: Article or HowTo (for step-based DIY posts) plus author and publisher, aligned with what Sana's theme already outputs. Do not add duplicate schema. Treat FAQ markup as optional reader content, not a rich-result target; recheck current Google guidance when producing.
- **Image briefs** following `friedmans-brand-guidelines` section 5 (imagery): the hero shows the actual project, shot in store or at a customer's home with consenting team members; until then, a clearly labeled, credited placeholder. Every image must explain something; if removing it loses nothing, drop it. Alt text describes the image.

## Step 8: Self-review before handoff

Read the full assembled draft (with product callouts and estimator placement) and record PASS or REVISE with a short passage-based reason for:

1. Useful to the stated reader, and the title's promise is delivered.
2. Steps are complete, correct and safe; tools and skill level stated.
3. Author fit and byline-swap test.
4. Facts verified in the ledger; no invented experience.
5. Callouts each add something; product callouts sit at the right step.
6. No prices or stock in copy; AP style; no dash punctuation.
7. Search package complete and accurate.
8. Brand check from `friedmans-brand-guidelines` section 8 passes.

Any REVISE: fix it and recheck the affected parts before handing off. Do not mark a draft ready because files exist or a checklist is filled; the judgment must come from reading the post.

## Output package (per post)

Under the post's content ID (format `FHI-YYYYMMDD-DEPT`, e.g. `FHI-20261020-GARDEN`):

- `article.md` and `article.html` (clean HTML with H2/H3, callout blocks and `{{product:ID}}` and `{{estimator}}` placeholders)
- `planning-record.json` (topic signals, candidates, PREP checks, outline)
- `seo.json` (fields above)
- `fact-ledger.json`
- `image-briefs.md`
- `review.json` (Step 8 results)

Then run `friedmans-product-estimator`, then `friedmans-sana-release`.
